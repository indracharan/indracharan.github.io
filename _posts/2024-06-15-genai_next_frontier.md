So much chaos around AI these days! "AI will replace me." "AI completed in minutes what would have taken me days." Blah, blah.

Well, AI is here to stay. I believe it will create more jobs and new opportunities than it will eliminate. Some existing roles will change, but entirely new kinds of work will emerge as people learn to build, manage, and work alongside these systems.

Even organizations that want to adopt AI often have no clear idea where it fits yet. They are eager to explore the technology, partly because they do not want to miss out on the next big thing. There is a real fear of missing out, or FOMO, around AI.

Let me explain this with an example.

When I was a child, I used to visit the bank with my mother. ATMs and digital payments were not part of our daily lives yet. To withdraw or deposit money, people had to visit the bank, pick up a withdrawal slip, and fill in details such as the account number and amount. The teller had a large ledger containing the details of the bank's account holders. Once the verification was complete, my mother received the money, and I always got a chocolate after making the FACE!

Coming back to the bank, every employee had to maintain records carefully. Since money was involved, every detail had to be accurate and checked, sometimes more than once. Bank employees were excellent at accounting and mathematics. Simple credit and debit calculations were at their fingertips, while I use a calculator for them now.

Then computers arrived. Credit and debit activities, calculations, transaction histories, and record-keeping gradually moved from paper to computer systems. In many cases, all it took was a simple press of the Enter key.

The teller who once took pride in doing every calculation might have wondered whether those skills were still useful and whether computers would take their job. This is similar to the fear many software engineers have about Generative AI today. 

Fast-forward to today: the introduction of computers did not create widespread unemployment. Instead, it created new jobs, opened new opportunities, and changed the way people worked. Some tasks became automated, but new responsibilities and roles emerged around the technology.

The same pattern will apply to Generative AI. It can write code that engineers once wrote entirely by hand. However, as of the time of writing this post, the generated code still needs careful review. AI outputs are not always deterministic, and models can produce incorrect or invented information, commonly referred to as hallucinations.

This does not mean that software engineers are no longer needed. Engineers still have to understand the problem, design the solution, review the output, test it, and take responsibility for what reaches production. As these tools improve, the role may shift from writing every line manually to working at a higher level and using AI effectively.

The lesson from the bank teller who learnt to use computers is simple: do not ignore the technology. Accept that AI is here to stay, learn how it works, and start using it thoughtfully in your daily work. The people who adapt may be in a better position to grow with the technology rather than compete against it.
